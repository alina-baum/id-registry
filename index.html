2026 BDBV Ebola Outbreak Tracker

Three outbreaks of two different viruses. BDBV is Bundibugyo virus, the species behind the 2026 outbreak in DRC and Uganda. EBOV is Zaire ebolavirus, the species behind both the 2014 West African and 2018 DRC epidemics.

The cumulative charts plot reported values; the daily incidence chart is derived from them.

Ervebo is Merck's rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine, developed and licensed against Zaire ebolavirus. Its efficacy against Bundibugyo virus is unknown and currently under trial.

True start dates are unknown for all three outbreaks. Shifting lets the 2026 overlay be varied; the historical curves stay anchored to their declaration dates.

Method and limitations

Handling of superseded values

Cumulative counts occasionally fell in the original reports, most visibly during WHO's November 2014 patient-database reconciliation, which first added roughly 3,800 previously uncounted cases and then removed several hundred. A reported point is kept only where no later report shows a lower cumulative value; contradicted interim values are dropped.

Case definitions differ between outbreaks

The 2026 series counts laboratory-confirmed cases only, excluding the large pool of suspected cases and suspected deaths recorded early in the outbreak. The 2014 and 2018 series follow their sources in combining confirmed and probable cases. Two different reporting systems are spliced within the 2014 series at day 288, where the patient-database figures run about 1.5 percent below the daily situation-report stream after duplicate removal.

Fatality ratios are not directly comparable across outbreaks

OutbreakCFR
2014 West Africa EBOV~40%
2018 DRC EBOV~66%
2026 DRC BDBV~48%, rising

These are crude ratios: cumulative reported deaths divided by cumulative reported cases. They should not be compared closely against one another. The denominators differ in what they count and in how completely cases were ever detected, and the 2026 figure is still moving because the outbreak is ongoing and many cases have not yet resolved.

Declaration dates are not true start dates

Every one of these outbreaks was already spreading before it was recognised, and in each case the true starting point is unknown. Aligning the curves on day zero is therefore a convention, not a measurement, and it favours whichever outbreak was detected latest in its course. The shift control moves the 2026 curve along the x-axis so its overlay against the historical curves can be varied.
